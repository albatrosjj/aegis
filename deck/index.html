The problem

Agents can pay. Trusting them with money is the hard part.

AI agents are getting wallets and paying for APIs, data, and other agents — autonomously. But a raw private key has no limits:

💉 One prompt injection

and a hijacked agent drains the whole wallet in 30 seconds.

🐛 One bad loop

and it spends the monthly budget on retries before anyone notices.

🔑 One leaked key

and there is no undo button on a blockchain.

Nobody wires a treasury to software with no spending controls.
Today, agent wallets are exactly that.

The agent never holds the money. The policy does.

Why Arc Network

The agent economy's money, native.

💵 USDC is the native asset

Gas, payments, budgets — one unit of account, no token juggling. Spend policies read exactly like a corporate card policy: in dollars.

⚡ Sub-second finality

Machine-speed payments need machine-speed settlement — and a circuit breaker that lands in seconds, not minutes.

🏛 Compliance-first stack

Enterprises will demand controls, auditability, and trust before wiring treasuries to agents. Circle's ecosystem is built on exactly that story — so is Aegis.

The analogy that already worked

Ramp & Brex, for the agent economy

Corporate cards existed for decades. Ramp and Brex turned spend control — limits, approvals, fraud detection — into multi-billion-dollar companies, because control is what unlocked giving cards to every employee.

Agent wallets are where corporate cards were in 1990:
powerful, and terrifying to hand out.

Aegis is the control layer that lets companies say yes — to every agent, with a budget, an allowlist, and a kill switch.
